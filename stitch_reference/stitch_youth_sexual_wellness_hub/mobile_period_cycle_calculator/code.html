Menstrual Health FAQ

A typical menstrual cycle lasts anywhere from 21 to 35 days for adult women, and up to 45 days for teenagers whose hormonal systems are still maturing. Bleeding usually lasts between 3 and 7 days.
Menstrual cycles respond quickly to changes in stress hormones (cortisol), school exam pressures, rapid weight changes, acute sickness, fasting periods, or rigorous exercise. Occasional slight variations of 2–4 days are normal.

Light spotting around ovulation (mid-cycle) can happen due to a temporary estrogen shift. However, persistent spotting after intercourse or heavy mid-cycle bleeding warrants a confidential review with a clinic nurse.
Seek guidance at a local youth-friendly clinic (e.g., FGAE clinics, Marie Stopes Ethiopia, or public health centers) if your bleeding soaks through more than one pad an hour for multiple consecutive hours, your pain disrupts daily function, or your period is absent for over 90 consecutive days without pregnancy.
